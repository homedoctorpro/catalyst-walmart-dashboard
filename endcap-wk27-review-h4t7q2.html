Why they weren't set — the four real drivers

1.The displays hadn't arrived yet — visits ran ahead of freight

Visits happened Aug 2–8, but at that point only 791 of 1,884 stores (42%) had received the endcap display. 545 were still in transit, 258 on order, and 290 were short-shipped. Freight timing is the single strongest predictor of whether an endcap got set:

Stores with the display in hand set at 41% — 2.5–5× the rate of stores still waiting on freight. The two "inventory" reasons are mostly this in disguise: 82% of "not enough inventory" stores and 71% of "product not located" stores had not received their display shipment. The field crews weren't wrong — the product genuinely wasn't there to build with.

2."No available space" is the structural ceiling — and it doesn't go away when freight lands

713 stores (38% of all visits) said there was no feature space available — the single largest bucket. Critically, this holds even where the display had arrived: 300 of 783 received stores (38%) still cited no space. Endcap/feature space in these stores is already committed to other programs, and store management controls it. Even with perfect freight execution, the current approach tops out around a ~40% set rate unless space is unlocked at the Walmart level.

3.In-store execution: product in the building that can't be found or trusted

Of the 261 "product not located" stores, 75 had actually received the display per shipment tracking — product is in the building but buried. The sub-reasons point at on-hand data integrity:

4.Store refusals are real but small

Only 74 stores (4.0%) refused the service — and most refusals are soft: 42 asked to complete at a later date, 24 were mid-remodel/inventory. These largely convert on a revisit.

What this means for recovery:

• 335 inventory-blocked stores have freight inbound (in transit or on order). At the received-store set rate (41%), a scheduled revisit after arrival converts roughly 136+ additional endcaps; if inventory was truly the only blocker, the upside is higher.

• 290 stores were short-shipped (245 of them not set) — these need a re-ship, not a revisit.

• The 713 no-space stores need a different lever: a directed feature from Walmart merchandising, or field teams returning when seasonal space frees up. Revisits alone won't move them.

• On-hand corrections should be filed for the ~220 stores with zero/incorrect on-hands so replenishment and future sets aren't suppressed.
